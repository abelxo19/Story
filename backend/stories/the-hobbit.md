## A Quiet Hill

![](/images/stories/the-hobbit/scene-1.svg)

In a round green hill, a small person lived a careful life. He liked breakfast, second breakfast, maps he never followed, and the comfort of a locked door. Visitors were rare, and that was how he liked it.

Then a wandering wizard knocked, and after him came a company of travelers with a plan too large for a quiet hill. They needed a burglar, they said. The hobbit said he was no burglar at all.

Still, by morning, a contract had been signed, a handkerchief packed, and the door of home stood behind him. The road, which had always been a pretty line on a map, was now dust under his feet.

## The Road and the Dark

![](/images/stories/the-hobbit/scene-2.svg)

The company crossed rivers, woods, and weather. Some nights were full of song. Some nights were full of fear. The hobbit learned that bravery is often only this: taking the next step when turning back would be easier.

Trolls, wolves, and mountain storms tested them. The wizard came and went like weather himself. The smallest traveler, who had wanted only tea, began to notice that the others looked to him when the path grew narrow.

## Riddles in the Dark

![](/images/stories/the-hobbit/scene-3.svg)

Far below the mountains, the hobbit became lost. In a cold lake lived a lonely creature who loved riddles more than company. They asked each other questions in the dark, and the prize was escape.

The hobbit found a small ring that seemed to want to be kept. He did not yet know what it would cost. He only knew that luck, or something like it, had given him a way out of the tunnels and back to the air.

## The Mountain's Heart

![](/images/stories/the-hobbit/scene-4.svg)

At last they came to a lonely mountain and the dragon who slept on a bed of gold. The hobbit went down where the others would not, saw the worm of fire, and brought back a single cup — and later, a secret that could end the siege.

Battle followed treasure, as it often does. When the smoke cleared, the hobbit wanted no crown and no hoard. He wanted his pantry, his armchair, and the sound of a kettle. He went home smaller in pride and larger in story, and the hill received him as if he had only been out for a long walk.
